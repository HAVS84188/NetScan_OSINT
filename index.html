System Purpose & Threat Analysis

Network Identity Tracking

Every time your device connects to a website, it must share its Public IP Address so the server knows where to send the website's data back to. However, this IP address is tied to your Internet Service Provider (ISP) and geographically links you to a specific city or neighborhood. Advertising networks and data brokers log this IP alongside your browsing habits to build a localized profile, tracking you as you move from site to site across the web.

Hardware & OS Profiling (Fingerprinting)

Browsers willingly hand over specific details about your device's hardware, ostensibly to help websites load the correct version of a page (like serving a mobile view versus a desktop view). But trackers exploit this. By combining your operating system version, the exact number of CPU cores your processor has, your system language, and your monitor's exact pixel resolution, they create a "fingerprint." Even if millions of people visit a site, it is highly likely that your specific combination of these hardware traits is entirely unique to you.

The "Do Not Track" Illusion

The "Do Not Track" (DNT) setting in your browser is nothing more than a polite digital request sent in the header of your web traffic. There is no legal or technical enforcement requiring a website to obey it. Today, the vast majority of ad networks, social media trackers, and analytic platforms completely ignore the DNT signal. If cookies are enabled, trackers will still drop small text files onto your hard drive to remember your identity and log your actions over months or even years.
